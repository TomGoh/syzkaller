### Does the negative counter correspond to real memory loss?
### 300 VM create/destroy cycles per arm, with drop_caches at every boundary to
### remove the page-cache drift that made a plain MemFree comparison unusable.

### VERDICT: INCONCLUSIVE. Recorded so nobody repeats it.
###
### The deltas swing by +-55 MB in both directions, in both arms, even with
### drop_caches at every boundary. The signal a two-page-per-VM loss would
### produce is 2400 kB per arm -- an order of magnitude below that noise.
### An earlier attempt without drop_caches (50 cycles per arm, three rounds)
### produced two mutually contradictory repetitions and was discarded.
###
### All this measurement can honestly support is that there is no LARGE leak.
### It cannot resolve two pages per VM, and no amount of repetition at this
### noise level will.
###
### The question is settled by the code instead, which is both cheaper and
### stronger -- the site that subtracts also frees:
###
###   arm.c:508-513   kvm_arch_vcpu_destroy()
###       atomic64_sub(vcpu->arch.stage2_mc.nr_pages << PAGE_SHIFT, ...);
###       free_hyp_memcache(&vcpu->arch.stage2_mc);
###
###   kvm_host.h:137-149  __free_hyp_memcache()
###       while (mc->nr_pages) { p = pop_hyp_memcache(...); free_fn(p, ...); }
###
### Every page counted out at teardown is a page handed back. The subtraction
### is correct; it is the matching addition that never happened, which is why
### the residual is negative rather than positive.
